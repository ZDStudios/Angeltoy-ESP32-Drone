01

Product Overview

The Angeltoy ESP32-Drone V2.0 is a miniature open-source quadcopter built around Espressif's ESP32-S2 microcontroller. It runs the official ESP-Drone firmware — an open-source project ported from the Bitcraze Crazyflie platform — and communicates entirely over 2.4 GHz WiFi. Your iPhone connects to the drone's WiFi hotspot and sends real-time flight commands through the ESP-Drone app. No RC transmitter, no pairing dongles.

The V2.0 board ships with firmware pre-flashed. For basic flight, you only need to install motors, install propellers, and attach a battery.

Your package may include a USB charger — if not, pick up a dedicated 1S LiPo USB charger (search "1S LiPo USB charger JST 1.25"). 03

Hardware Assembly

Assembly is straightforward — no soldering required for V2.0. The motors press-fit into the motor mounts on the PCB frame. The most critical part is getting the propeller directions correct.

Top-Down View — Motor Positions & Rotation Directions
CCW — Counter-clockwise (Type A prop)
CW — Clockwise (Type B prop)

Step-by-Step Assembly

1
Inspect the board

Lay the PCB flat. Identify the four motor mount sockets in the corners — they are labeled M1, M2, M3, M4 directly on the silkscreen. Confirm the FRONT side (usually marked on the PCB with an arrow or "FRONT" text).

2
Press-fit the motors

Push each motor into its corresponding socket. The motor shaft points upward. Motors are typically color-coded — check if red motors go on M1/M4 (CCW) and blue/black on M2/M3 (CW), but verify against the board's labels. Route the motor wires through the arm channel if provided.

3
Identify your propellers

Propellers are marked Type A (or "A" stamped on the hub) for CCW rotation, and Type B (or "B") for CW rotation. Check the text molded into the blade hub. Do NOT skip this step — reversed props cause a crash on first takeoff.

4
Install propellers in the correct positions

Using the diagram above:
M1 (front-left) → Type A / CCW
M2 (front-right) → Type B / CW
M3 (back-left) → Type B / CW
M4 (back-right) → Type A / CCW

Press each prop firmly onto the motor shaft until it clicks or seats fully. If they use tiny screws, tighten gently — don't overtighten.

5
Verify propeller pitch (quick visual check)

Hold the drone at eye level. A CCW propeller's leading edge (thicker edge) curves to the left when viewed from above. A CW propeller's leading edge curves to the right. Confirm this matches the motor positions before proceeding.

6
Attach the battery

Connect your 300 mAh 1S LiPo to the JST 1.25 mm connector on the board. The connector is polarized — it only fits one way. Do NOT force it. Tuck the battery into the battery bay (usually on the underside of the PCB). A small piece of foam or velcro holds it securely during flight.

🚨
Wrong Propeller Direction = Instant Crash

Propeller direction is the #1 assembly mistake. If even one prop is reversed, the drone will flip immediately on takeoff and potentially break props or motors. Double-check every position against the diagram before your first flight.

04

iPhone App Installation

The drone is controlled using the official ESP-Drone app by Espressif Systems. It's free on the App Store.

1
Open App Store on your iPhone

Tap the App Store icon. Make sure you're connected to the internet (not the drone's WiFi yet).

2
Search "ESP-Drone"

In the search tab, type ESP-Drone. Look for the app published by Espressif Systems (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. — the official publisher. It has a blue/teal drone icon.

3
Download and install

Tap Get → install. The app is free, roughly 10–20 MB. It requires iOS 14 or later.

4
Allow Local Network access

On first launch, iOS will ask if ESP-Drone can access devices on your local network. Tap Allow — this is required for the UDP communication between your iPhone and the drone.

ℹ️
App Source Code

The iOS app is fully open source: github.com/EspressifApps/ESP-Drone-iOS. If you want to modify the app or build it yourself, clone that repo and open in Xcode.

05

Connecting via WiFi

The drone acts as a WiFi Access Point (AP). Your iPhone connects to the drone's network directly — not your home router. The drone creates its own hotspot when powered on.

WiFi Credentials

SSID (Network Name) ESP-DRONE_XXXX
Password 12345678
Security WPA2

XXXX in the SSID is the last four characters of the drone's MAC address. Example: ESP-DRONE_A3F2. It will be unique to your board.

1
Power on the drone

Connect the battery (if not already). The blue LED on the board should blink, indicating the firmware is running and the WiFi AP is active. Give it 3–5 seconds to fully initialise.

2
Connect iPhone to the drone's WiFi

Go to Settings → WiFi on your iPhone. You'll see a network starting with ESP-DRONE_. Tap it, enter password 12345678, and connect. Your iPhone will show "No Internet Connection" — this is normal. The drone provides WiFi, not internet.

3
Keep WiFi connected

iOS may ask "Keep using this network?" because it has no internet. Tap "Use Without Internet" or "Keep" to stay connected to the drone. Do not switch to your home WiFi during flight.

4
Open the ESP-Drone app

Launch the ESP-Drone app. It will automatically detect the drone on the network and show the connection status. A green indicator or "Connected" status means you're ready.

📡
WiFi Range is Short (~10–15 metres)

The ESP32-S2's WiFi range in AP mode is limited to roughly 10–15 metres in open space — less through walls. Stay within this range while flying. Flying too far causes the app to lose connection and the drone will hover or descend.

06

First Flight

🏠
Fly Indoors First

For your first flight, choose a large indoor space with no ceiling fans and clear floor area. This drone has no GPS — outdoors it will drift with any wind. Master hovering indoors first.

1
Place drone on a flat surface, FRONT facing away from you

Orientation matters. The FRONT of the drone should point away. In Mode 2, pushing the right stick forward (pitch) moves the drone away from you. If the drone is backwards, your controls will feel inverted.

2
Open the app, confirm connection

You should see green/active status. Check that your iPhone is still connected to ESP-DRONE_XXXX WiFi.

3
Arm the motors (throttle down + yaw right)

In the app, push the left stick fully down and to the right simultaneously for ~2 seconds. The motors should start to spin slowly — this indicates the drone is armed and ready to fly. Stand clear of the propellers.

4
Slowly raise the left stick to take off

Gradually push the left stick (Throttle) upward from the bottom. The drone will lift at roughly 50–60% throttle. Use small, gentle inputs. Hovering requires constant small corrections with the right stick (Pitch/Roll).

5
Land: reduce throttle slowly

Gently lower the left stick. As the drone descends and touches down, push the left stick fully down to disarm the motors. The props will stop spinning.

🔋
Flight Time

A 300 mAh 1S battery typically gives 3–5 minutes of flight. The board has low-battery detection — the LED will flash faster when the battery is low. Land immediately when you see this. Flying on a depleted LiPo damages the battery permanently.

07

Flight Modes

ESP-Drone supports three flight modes. The V2.0 board supports Stabilise mode out of the box. Height-hold and Position-hold require optional expansion boards (sold separately).

ModeDescriptionHardware RequiredAvailable on V2.0?
Stabilise Keeps the drone level using the IMU (gyroscope + accelerometer). You control throttle, pitch, roll, and yaw manually. Main board only (MPU-6050) ✓ Yes — default
Height-hold Automatically maintains altitude at a set height. Useful for beginners. Throttle stick controls altitude target, not direct thrust. MS5611 barometric pressure module (ext. board) Optional add-on
Position-hold Holds X/Y position as well as altitude. Drone hovers in place without drifting — great for photography. PMW3901 optical flow + VL53L1X ToF sensor (ext. board) Optional add-on
🔁
Switching Flight Modes in the App

In the ESP-Drone app, a mode toggle button switches between available flight modes. If you only have the base board, only Stabilise mode will work. Attempting Height-hold without the barometer board will result in unstable altitude behaviour.

⚙️
Tuning Trim for Hover Drift

If the drone drifts forward when hovering with sticks centred, increase the Pitch Trim slightly in a negative direction. If it drifts right, adjust Roll Trim negative. Small increments of 0.5° at a time. This is normal — small manufacturing variations in motor placement cause slight imbalances.

Pre-flight Checklist

Run through this before every flight session.

✓ All 4 propellers are firmly seated — no wobble, no looseness
✓ Propeller directions match the diagram (CCW on M1/M4, CW on M2/M3)
✓ Battery is fully charged (check your charger's indicator light)
✓ Battery connector is fully seated — not loose or partially disconnected
✓ Battery is secured to the frame — won't shift during flight
✓ iPhone is connected to ESP-DRONE_XXXX WiFi
✓ App shows connection established (green status)
✓ Drone is on a flat surface, FRONT facing away
✓ Flying area is clear of obstacles within 2 metres
⚠ No ceiling fans, loose cables, or pets in the flight area
⚠ Battery has not been flown in the last 10 minutes (LiPo needs to cool)

Troubleshooting

Drone flips immediately on takeoff
Almost certainly a reversed propeller. Power off immediately. Re-check every prop position against the diagram in Section 3. The most common mistake is swapping Type A (CCW) and Type B (CW) props. Also confirm the motors themselves spin the correct direction by briefly powering the drone on a bench and observing which way each motor turns before props are installed.
I can't find ESP-DRONE_XXXX in my WiFi list
Check that the battery is connected and the board's LED is blinking (not solid or off). If the LED is off, the board isn't powered — check the battery connector. If the LED is solid, the firmware may have crashed — disconnect and reconnect the battery to reboot. Wait 5–10 seconds after power-on before scanning WiFi. Move your iPhone closer to the drone when scanning.
App connects but controls don't respond / drone doesn't arm
Make sure you performed the arm gesture correctly: left stick fully down-and-right for ~2 seconds. Check that iOS didn't switch you back to your home WiFi network in the background — this happens when iOS decides the drone's network "has no internet". Go to Settings → WiFi, confirm you're still on ESP-DRONE_XXXX. Also try enabling Airplane Mode + WiFi only to prevent iOS from auto-switching.
Drone drifts badly in one direction when hovering
Some drift is normal without position-hold mode. Use the Roll Trim and Pitch Trim sliders in the app settings (Section 8) to compensate. Adjust in 0.5° increments and re-test. Also check that the battery is centred on the frame — off-centre weight shifts the centre of gravity.
Only 2 or 3 motors spin
A motor may not be fully seated in its socket. Power off, press each motor down firmly until it clicks. Also check that the motor wire isn't kinked or broken at the strain relief point. If a motor is confirmed dead, replacement coreless motors matching the original size (typically 0720 or 0820 coreless) are available cheaply on AliExpress.
Very short flight time (under 2 minutes)
The battery may be degraded or deeply discharged. Always charge immediately after flight. If the battery voltage drops below 3.0 V per cell (1S = 3.0 V total), the chemistry is damaged. Purchase a replacement 300 mAh 1S LiPo. Also check that no prop is rubbing on the prop guard — increased friction drains the battery faster.
The ESP-Drone app crashes or freezes
Force-close the app and re-open. Make sure your iPhone is running iOS 14 or later. If the issue persists, delete and re-install the app from the App Store. Check the GitHub repo (EspressifApps/ESP-Drone-iOS) for any known iOS version issues.

Reflashing Firmware (if needed)

The firmware targets ESP-IDF release/v5.0 or later.

1
Install ESP-IDF

Follow the ESP-IDF Getting Started guide for your OS. Install the release/v5.0 branch specifically: git clone -b release/v5.0 https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf.git

2
Clone the ESP-Drone repository

git clone https://github.com/espressif/esp-drone.git --recursive

3
Set the target chip

For V2.0 with ESP32-S2: idf.py set-target esp32s2

4
Build and flash

Connect the board via USB-C (hold BOOT button while connecting to enter flash mode). Run: idf.py build flash monitor -p /dev/ttyUSB0 (replace with your serial port).

PC Debugging with cfclient

cfclient is a Python desktop app from the Crazyflie project that works with ESP-Drone over WiFi. It lets you tune PID parameters, monitor sensor data live, and control the drone with a USB gamepad.

1
Install cfclient (Python required)

git clone https://github.com/qljz1993/crazyflie-clients-python.git
Then: cd crazyflie-clients-python && pip3 install -e . && cfclient

2
Connect your PC to the drone's WiFi

Same credentials: SSID ESP-DRONE_XXXX, password 12345678. Then in cfclient, select the WiFi connection type and scan.

🎮
Extension Boards (Optional Upgrades)

To unlock Height-hold mode: add the MS5611 pressure module extension board via I2C.
To unlock Position-hold mode: add the PMW3901 optical flow + VL53L1X ToF module, mounted on the underside facing the ground.
The HMC5883 compass module adds head-free mode for outdoor use.
Search these on AliExpress as "ESP-Drone expansion" boards — they use the same edge-connector form factor.
